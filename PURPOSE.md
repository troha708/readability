# Readability — Statement of Purpose

## What the app is for

Readability exists so that an ordinary reader actually reads the Bible — all of it — and
understands what they read as they go. Every feature serves one of those two goals. Reading:
a clear path through 1,189 chapters, progress that persists, a habit that builds. Understanding:
context supplied at the moment it's needed, in plain English, and a quiz at the end of each
chapter so the reading sticks.

## Who it's for

The reader, not the researcher. The notes are written for a curious adult meeting a passage for
the first time: someone who doesn't know what a Levite is, has never heard of the Septuagint,
and shouldn't have to. Curiosity and intelligence are assumed; background never is.

This is a real choice with a real cost. A reader who wants close textual analysis — parsing
Greek tenses, weighing manuscript families verse by verse — is better served by a technical
commentary, and the notes don't try to be one. The app keeps deeper tools a tap away for a
reader whose appetite grows (word study, cross-references, translation comparison), but the
reading surface stays plain.

## What the notes are

A reading companion. A note's job is to hand the reader what the text assumes but doesn't say —
what a word meant, who a group was, what custom is in view, how the passage fits the larger
story — so the passage itself lands. Historical and linguistic material appears when it unlocks
the passage's sense, and only then. It is a means, not the point. A note that merely retells the
verses, or displays learning for its own sake, has failed.

## What the notes are not

- **Not a commentary or study reference.** They don't survey scholarship or argue positions;
  they equip a single reading of the text in front of the reader.
- **Not devotional.** They never moralize, sermonize, or tell the reader what to feel.
- **Not aligned to a tradition.** On questions Christians have long debated, the notes lay out
  the main readings and don't pick a side.
- **Not a replacement for the text.** Scripture is the page; notes live behind a lightbulb and
  open by choice. If a note competes with the text for attention, the note is wrong.

## Commitments

1. **Nothing untrue.** Every factual-sounding sentence must actually be true. Debated claims are
   attributed ("often read as…"); overstated color is verified, hedged, or cut.
2. **Receipts.** Claims are checked against established public-domain commentaries and
   translators' notes, with per-claim references kept alongside the notes for any reader who
   wants to verify. The references serve the reader's trust; they are not there to make the
   notes look scholarly.
3. **Honesty about how it's made.** Drafted with AI, reviewed by a human, edited against a
   written style guide — and the app says so plainly.
4. **Reading is free.** No account, no ads, no paywall between a person and the text.

## The test

For any note, feature, or design decision: does it help someone keep reading, and understand
what they just read? If not, it doesn't belong, however well made.
